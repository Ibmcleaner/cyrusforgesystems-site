Cyrus Insight Engine

Turn historical measurements into an estimate of what may happen next, explain the factors behind that estimate, and compare it with the eventual result.

The engine is a repeatable statistical workflow: data preparation, a defined model, confidence estimates, publication and evaluation. Its current implementation is specialized; applying the workflow to a business problem requires a suitable dataset, model and validation.

How the engine works

  1. 1. Define the question and collect the inputs

    Start with an outcome that can be measured later, a forecast horizon, and a clear cutoff for information. Historical observations establish the baseline; contextual inputs describe conditions that may change the result. External reference values can provide a comparison point.

    The current pipeline gathers historical records and contextual data, then combines them with supplied reference lines. Missing values must remain visible rather than being filled with invented facts.

  2. 2. Establish a baseline and apply defined adjustments

    A baseline summarizes past performance. Recent observations can receive more weight, while limited histories are pulled toward a broader average to reduce overreaction to small samples. Contextual adjustments then account for relevant conditions.

    The current implementation uses fixed statistical methods and coefficients. It is not allowed to retune itself each week. A new business application would need its own predictors, fitted parameters and checks; the existing coefficients are not universal.

  3. 3. Express uncertainty and explain the estimate

    The output includes a numerical projection or probability, a confidence category, and the inputs supporting it. Where a reference value is available, the engine compares its estimate with that benchmark.

    The current explanation system fills templates with the same numbers used by the model. It does not ask a language model to invent a narrative. Missing information or an unresolved condition can produce a recheck or pass rather than an unsupported conclusion. A confidence label is an estimate, not proof that the model is calibrated.

  4. 4. Publish a dated version

    The pipeline produces a website data file and a report, and records projections in a ledger. Version timing matters: an estimate made after the outcome is known cannot be treated as a forecast.

    The documented workflow commits the website data to GitHub, which triggers the site's Cloudflare deployment. The publication page shows the data's update information. This is scheduled processing, not a model recalculating every time a visitor opens the page.

  5. 5. Compare the forecast with the observed outcome

    A later grading step uses observed results to evaluate eligible published projections. The record groups results by output type and confidence category so readers can inspect where estimates perform well or poorly.

    The rules specify which published version counts and when an observation is excluded. Small samples, missing observations and data quality limit the conclusions. The current player model has not been backtested against historical player reference lines.

Applications Across Industries

The same workflow could support demand forecasting, staffing and workload planning, inventory estimates or service-volume projections. For example, a contact center could estimate next week's hourly arrivals from its historical traffic and known events, log the forecast, and compare it with actual arrivals.

Those business integrations are potential adaptations, not existing features of this demonstration. Each needs relevant data, domain-specific assumptions and evaluation before operational use.

Worked example · NFL player statistics

Applying the Insight Engine to player projections

This implementation uses NFL player statistics to demonstrate two measurable outcomes: quarterback passing yards and the probability that a running back scores at least one rushing or receiving touchdown. These are the example's measures, not the engine's product name.

Inputs and calculation

Passing yards: The model uses an exponentially weighted average of prior starts, with a half-life of eight games, shrunk toward the league average. It applies an opponent pass-defense factor and adjustments fitted on 2021–2025 starts for home field, division games, a dome, a missing top target, the spread and the game total.

Its estimate of the chance of finishing above or below the reference line uses a 50/50 blend of the projection and that line, with an assumed spread of 30% of the mean. This makes the estimate market-informed; it is not independent of that reference.

Touchdown probability: A shrunk per-game scoring rate is scaled by the team's implied points. The chance of at least one touchdown is calculated as 1 − exp(−λ), where λ is the estimated touchdown rate. The documented categories are Strong at 70% or higher, Moderate from 60% to below 70%, and Slight below 60%.

Publication and evaluation

The documented schedule runs Wednesday and Friday at 6:45 PM Central. The last projection published before kickoff counts for grading. Grading starts no earlier than 12 hours after kickoff using weekly player statistics; a missing player statistics row when the team played is treated as void under the current rules. The public record begins with 2026 Week 8.
