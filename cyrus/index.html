How the NFL Predictor works

  1. 01 · DATA

    Start from the numbers

    Public game and player stats, injury reports, weather and the schedule, using only what was known before kickoff.

  2. 02 · CALL

    Make the call

    Each player gets a projection: the chance he scores, or how many yards he throws, with a confidence level.

  3. 03 · COMPARE

    Compare with the market

    Our number sits next to the market line. The bigger the gap, the stronger the label. When the gap is too small or a key fact is missing, it's a Pass.

Beyond football: where this engine works

The NFL Predictor is a public test of a general system. Strip away the football and what's left is a forecasting loop any business can use: pull the data automatically, make a projection, log it before the outcome is known, grade it against what actually happened, and keep the score in the open. Football is simply the hardest place to hide a bad model, because the answers arrive every Sunday.

Call and ticket volume

Project next week's contact-center or help-desk demand by day and hour, then grade it against the actuals, so staffing decisions rest on a model with a known accuracy.

Sales and demand

Forecast orders, bookings or inventory needs from your own history plus the outside factors that move them, with every forecast scored after the fact.

Operational risk

Estimate which jobs, shipments or accounts are most likely to miss a deadline or go overdue, so attention goes where the probability is highest.

Any number you argue about

If a metric gets predicted in meetings, it can be predicted by a logged, graded model instead.

What you get is not a prediction, it's a track record: a system that says how often it has been right, so you know how much to trust it. Tell us the number you want to predict.
